Three changes, all visible in the phone above — a split beat before the answer (step 5), no ✗ on your own choice ever, and an explanation that spends its middle paragraph on why so many of us picked A. Plus a hint you can pull yourself, which is a proposed AC-48 amendment.

Rebuilt on the D-7 spine. The prior state-explorer is phone-first too, but its vocabulary is a round — next question, end quiz, a question counter. One question in five minutes deletes all of it. What survives, and is reused here, is its edge-state catalogue.

This direction now looks strongest on the new evidence, and the reason is narrow: it is the only one where a newcomer can commit, change their mind, and ask for help without anyone knowing they did any of it. Anonymity is the floor. The split beat is what turns it into belonging.

This is also the expensive direction. The only one that needs Stories B1, B5, B6 and B7 in full: a join path, a 200-concurrent deadline write burst (AC-52–55, the highest-risk moment in the system), and canary-secret proof that no pre-reveal payload carries the answer.

What else it buys. A real anonymous count rather than the host's thumb on a counter, a per-person recap computed on-device and never transmitted (AC-58), and code you can hold six inches from your face — the only version of AC-32 a back row can actually pass.
