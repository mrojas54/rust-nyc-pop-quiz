Why this exists — §9's private pace had nowhere left to live

T-15 gave the per-participant trace stepper to Direction B for a §9 reason: a newcomer should be able to work through it at their own pace, with nobody watching how many times they went back. A-4 then put the source and the trace on the wall only, host-stepped — and with the explanation spoken aloud, nothing on the participant's side was re-readable. That property was gone from the product.

Every fix considered on the phone traded private pace against the room's attention: prose on fifty phones during the reveal is fifty people not looking at the wall or at each other, in the ninety seconds the room is supposed to be working through it together. This page makes no such trade — it moves the private pace off the phone and out of the segment. The room stays a room. It is also the literal form of the one thing: placed last, nobody has to stop it — it walks out with them.

What this page deliberately does not have

No room state, of any kind. Client call: the page is informational. It does not say what the room chose, what you chose, how many got it right, or that 24 people read dedup as unique that night. AC-56 holds unamended — no per-person record outlives the room and the totals expire with it. The split is the most §9 fact the segment produces and it stays in the room that produced it.

Open, and it is a real conflict: AC-95 requires the count. "The explanation names the most-chosen incorrect option, states how many chose it, and says why it is a reasonable reading." On this page the middle beat names the popular wrong answer but cannot state how many chose it without carrying room state. The reading here is that AC-95 was written for the live reveal, where the count is the mechanism — you are not alone — and that a page read alone days later needs a different form of the same beat: make the wrong reading reasonable as a fact about the language, not about that night's room. That scoping is not yet a criterion. It needs either an amendment to AC-95 or a sibling criterion for the take-it-home surface, and it is tone-architect's to settle.

Rotation is the expiry, and it needs no mechanism. The client asked for something that is "not set for all time — maybe it expires until the next meetup." A URL that always means the last meetup's question gets that by construction: no expiry job, no archive, no dated URLs to accumulate, and only ever one question public at a time.
