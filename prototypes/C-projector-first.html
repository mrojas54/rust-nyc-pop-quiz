What this direction is testing
Client testimony, 2026-08-14 Reworked against the room's own feedback: everything is very intimidating; people aren't allowed to be wrong. The buzzer no longer grades you — no ✓, no ✗. At the split and the reveal it shows one number: how many people said what you said. The verdict is on the projector, about the answer, never about the person. A quiet pull-your-own hint is proposed here too.

AC-91 becomes a typography problem. Thirty seconds to form an opinion is the whole budget the format allows. Here that is decided by one thing — how big the code is set on one screen — instead of by fifty different phones held at fifty distances.

The explanation is spoken, not read. B puts the prose on every phone; C puts it only in the host's hand and on the projector's receipt. Everyone hears it at the same time, which is the version that actually spreads the understanding — the people who have it telling the people who don't, rather than two people debating in front of everyone. It also means AC-42 ("reads well aloud") stops being aspirational and becomes the only way the explanation is ever delivered.

What it costs. Someone in the back who cannot read the screen has no fallback — there is no code on their phone. AC-32's "legible without pinch-zoom on a 375px phone" has no surface to apply to, and would need withdrawing or rewriting against the projector instead.

What it buys. The join path and write burst still exist, but the participant client is five buttons — the smallest thing that can still produce a real anonymous count, and the least that can go wrong in front of the room.
